Learn

How your brain and body respond to stress. Educational only.

Amygdala

The alarm system

Scans for possible threats and can trigger a fast stress response, sometimes before you have consciously thought about it.

Hippocampus

The memory hub

Helps form memories and puts experiences in context. It also helps the brain judge whether a situation is actually safe.

Prefrontal cortex

The thinking centre

Supports planning, focus and decision-making, and helps regulate emotional reactions from deeper brain areas.

The HPA axis

A simplified view of the body's main stress pathway.

  1. HypothalamusDetects a stressor and releases CRH
  2. →
  3. Pituitary glandReleases ACTH into the blood
  4. →
  5. Adrenal glandsRelease cortisol

Cortisol also feeds back to the brain, helping switch the response off once the pressure passes.

Body responses

Sympathetic nervous system

The "fight-or-flight" branch. It raises heart rate and breathing to prepare you to act.

Adrenaline and noradrenaline

Released within seconds. They increase heart rate and alertness, which is why your heart can pound and your hands can feel shaky.

Cortisol

Released more slowly via the HPA axis. It helps the body mobilise energy, and its levels normally settle once the pressure passes.

Short-term and prolonged stress

Short-term

A normal, helpful response

A short burst of stress can sharpen attention and help you act, for example before an exam or a presentation. It usually settles once the situation is over.

Prolonged

When there is little time to recover

When pressure stays high for a long time without enough recovery, many people notice things like poor sleep, tension or low mood. How strongly this happens differs from person to person.

Recovery

Sleep

Regular sleep supports memory, concentration and emotional balance.

Movement

Gentle movement, such as a short walk, helps many people feel calmer.

Social support

Talking with people you trust can make pressure feel more manageable.

This page is general education. MindSpace cannot measure what is happening in your brain or body, and it cannot diagnose anything. Your ratings in Track are your own perceptions, and they are meant to help you notice patterns, not to label you.
